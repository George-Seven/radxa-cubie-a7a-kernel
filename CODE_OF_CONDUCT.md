# Code of conduct

This is a small project maintained by one person. The rules are short.

## What is expected

**Be decent to each other.** People here are at very different levels — some are
kernel developers, some are flashing their first SD card. Both are welcome, and
neither should be made to feel stupid for asking.

**Assume good faith.** If someone reports a bug that turns out to be their own
misconfiguration, that is a normal outcome, not a waste of anyone's time. If
someone's suggestion seems obvious or naive, answer the question rather than the
tone.

**Be accurate about what you know.** If you are guessing, say you are guessing.
A confident wrong answer costs more time than no answer, especially with
hardware, where a bad instruction can leave someone with an unbootable board.

**Say when you are corrected.** That applies to me as much as anyone. This
project has published figures that turned out to be wrong, and they were
corrected in public with the reasoning rather than quietly edited.

## What is not

- Personal attacks, harassment, or discrimination of any kind.
- Dismissing someone for their level of experience, their hardware, or their
  language.
- Posting anyone's personal information.
- Deliberately misleading advice, or instructions you have not verified, given
  as though you had.

## Scope

This applies to issues, pull requests, discussions and any other space
associated with this project.

## If something goes wrong

Open an issue, or contact the maintainer through GitHub. Reports will be handled
privately where that is appropriate.

I would rather resolve something with a conversation than a ban, but I will
remove comments, close threads, or block accounts if it comes to that — and the
threshold is lower for behaviour aimed at someone else than for behaviour aimed
at me.
